Growise (Airconcur) · AI Module Developer → AI Engineer · 2023 – present

Multi-LLM RAG Modules

The problem

Several AI products needed to answer questions over customer-specific data, with LLMs and embedding models tailored per client, and without ever mixing data between users.

How it works (standard RAG pattern)

Documents→Chunk & embed→Per-user vector index→Retrieve & rerank→LLM answer with context

What I built

  • Containerized, reusable RAG modules with pluggable LLMs and embedding models, reused across application domains.
  • Isolated, user-specific index pipelines to prevent cross-user data leakage and data mismatch.
  • Integrations with Pinecone, ChromaDB and MongoDB.
  • Later re-engineered the retrieval layer: moved to Voyage AI embeddings, added a reranker, and cleaned, de-duplicated, merged and canonicalized the stored data so it is richer and unique.

Results

  • ~30% lower retrieval latency vs. my first version, from vector-indexing optimization
  • ~60% higher reasoning accuracy vs. the unoptimized version, from the retrieval re-engineering plus context, prompt and inference-setting optimization
  • Up to 50% higher response accuracy vs. generic prompts, using task-specific prompt design

Undergraduate thesis · ITS Surabaya · Published in Jurnal Teknik ITS, 2021

Handwashing Motion Classification with CNN

The problem

During the COVID-19 pandemic, many people still washed their hands too quickly or skipped steps, and nobody can watch public sinks 24/7. The goal: use a camera and deep learning to recognize each handwashing step and check whether it was done properly.

How it works

Handwashing video→Frame extraction→Data augmentation→EfficientNet-B0 classifier→Moving-average smoothing→Labeled output video

Demo video

Left: raw input. Right: the predicted handwashing step written on each frame. Watch on YouTube ↗

What I did

  • Cleaned the public Kaggle Hand Wash Dataset (mislabeled and mixed-motion clips) and extended it with my own recordings using a webcam and a DSLR on new backgrounds.
  • Trained a classifier on EfficientNet-B0 with NoisyStudent weights, with augmentation before the base model.
  • Added moving-average smoothing over frame predictions and tuned the window size to reduce flickering labels.
  • Tested robustness against camera field of view, camera angle and sink background.

Results

  • 99% accuracy in the training phase, above 90% on evaluation in under 20 epochs
  • 12/12 handwashing steps recognized in testing, with 2/12 inconsistent predictions
  • 2 s moving-average window gave the best results
  • Limits: a 30° camera angle and unseen backgrounds lowered accuracy, pointing to the need for a more diverse dataset and careful camera placement
PythonTensorFlowEfficientNet-B0Computer visionData augmentation
