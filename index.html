Handwashing Motion Classification (published paper)

A CNN (EfficientNet-B0) that recognizes handwashing steps from a camera feed: 12/12 steps recognized in testing. With video demo.

Jurnal Teknik ITS · 2021

How it works

  1. Handwashing video
  2. Frame extraction
  3. Data augmentation
  4. EfficientNet-B0 classifier
  5. Moving-average smoothing
  6. Labeled output video

The problem

During the COVID-19 pandemic, many people still washed their hands too quickly or skipped steps, and nobody can watch public sinks 24/7. The goal: use a camera and deep learning to recognize each handwashing step and check whether it was done properly.

Demo video

Left: raw input. Right: the predicted handwashing step written on each frame. Watch on YouTube

What I did

  • Cleaned the public Kaggle Hand Wash Dataset (mislabeled and mixed-motion clips) and extended it with my own recordings using a webcam and a DSLR on new backgrounds.
  • Trained a classifier on EfficientNet-B0 with NoisyStudent weights, with augmentation before the base model.
  • Added moving-average smoothing over frame predictions and tuned the window size to reduce flickering labels.
  • Tested robustness against camera field of view, camera angle and sink background.

Results

  • 99% accuracy in the training phase, above 90% on evaluation in under 20 epochs
  • 12/12 handwashing steps recognized in testing, with 2/12 inconsistent predictions
  • 2 s moving-average window gave the best results
  • Limits: a 30° camera angle and unseen backgrounds lowered accuracy, pointing to the need for a more diverse dataset and careful camera placement
PythonTensorFlowEfficientNet-B0Computer visionData augmentation
